abstract: The growing low-altitude economy makes unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) operations a pivotal asset for urban mobility, yet sharing airspace among multiple competing operators poses a challenge to commercial privacy. While centralized strategic management ensures flight safety, a single centralized cost model cannot satisfy operators' distinct commercial preferences. This study proposes a two-tier decentralized strategic management aligned with the FAA's concept of operations. Operators plan locally using a cooperative heuristic algorithm shaped by each operator's own commercial preference, while a service provider arbitrates cross-operator conflicts through a rule-based protocol without observing those preferences. In a city-scale simulation with three heterogeneous operators, no uniformly imposed weight profile under centralized management can satisfy all operators, whereas the decentralized one satisfies each operator accordingly. To our knowledge, this is the first framework to investigate cross-operator conflict resolution without observing operators' commercial preferences, thereby bridging engineering feasibility and commercial viability for future UAV traffic management.
